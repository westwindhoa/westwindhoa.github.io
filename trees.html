Removing a Tree

Westwind's governing documents have exactly one rule about trees. Read below what our governing documents say about trees, and what Montgomery County separately requires.

Straight from the Declaration

Article VII, Section 2

This is the entire tree rule in Westwind's Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions, word for word. Nothing else in the Declaration, Bylaws, Articles of Incorporation, or the 2012 ACC Guidelines mentions trees.

“No fence, wall, tree, hedge or shrub planting shall be maintained in such manner as to obstruct sight lines for vehicular traffic. Except as may be required to comply with the prior sentence, no tree of a diameter of more than four inches measured two feet above ground level, lying without the approved building driveway and parking areas, shall be removed without the approval of the Architectural Control Committee.” Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions · Article VII, Section 2
In plain English

Do You Need ACC Approval to Remove a Tree?

Two things matter: how thick the trunk is and whether the tree sits inside or outside your approved driveway and parking footprint. There is no separate rule for front yards versus back yards anywhere in the governing documents.

Trunk under 4" diameter (measured 2 ft up) No approval needed, anywhere on the lot
Trunk over 4", inside the approved driveway/parking area No approval needed, carved out by the covenant
Trunk over 4", anywhere else on the lot, front or back ACC approval required before removal

No Replacement Requirement

Neither the Declaration nor the 2012 ACC Guidelines say anything about replanting a tree once it's removed. The Guidelines cover fences, sheds, shutters, and gutters in detail, but they don't repeat the Declaration's tree rule or add a replacement obligation. Based on the written documents alone, there is no requirement to plant a new tree in place of one that's removed.

A Note on Past Guidance

If you were previously told a different rule applies, for example, a separate standard for front-yard trees, or a requirement to plant a replacement, that guidance doesn't match what's actually recorded in the governing documents. This page reflects a direct reading of the Declaration itself. If the Board has an informal practice alongside the written rule (such as routinely asking for street-facing trees to be flagged, or encouraging but not requiring replacement plantings), that would exist separately from the covenant and should be confirmed with the Board directly.

The One Rule That Applies Regardless of Size

Separately from the removal rule, no tree, hedge, shrub, fence, or wall may be maintained, regardless of size, in a way that blocks sight lines for vehicular traffic. This is a standing maintenance obligation, not a removal trigger, and it applies whether or not the tree is otherwise exempt from the ACC approval requirement.

Removing a tree that needs approval?

Submit It Through the ACC

Tree removal that requires approval goes through the same process as any other exterior modification.

A separate layer of rules

Montgomery County's Tree Rules

The ACC covenant above is Westwind's own rule. Montgomery County has its own, separate set of tree rules that apply on top of it, and the county's own guidance explicitly says to check with your HOA as well, so both layers apply together.

Most homeowners

Trees on Your Own Lot

The county generally does not require a permit to remove a tree on private property. Removing a stump on private property doesn't need a county permit either.

Larger projects only

When County Rules Kick In

The county's Tree Canopy Law only applies if your project already requires a sediment control permit. This generally means new construction, disturbing more than 5,000 sq ft of land, or moving 100+ cubic yards of earth. Removing one or two trees on their own typically doesn't trigger this.

Trees near the street

Roadside & Right-of-Way Trees

A tree growing in the public right-of-way (between the street and your property, not on your lot) needs two permits, one from Montgomery County and one from the Maryland DNR Forest Service, before it can be pruned, disturbed, or removed.

Rare exceptions

Historic & Champion Trees

Trees in a designated historic area, or listed on the county's Champion Tree Register, need separate clearance before any work. Uncommon for a typical Westwind lot, but worth knowing.

Safety first

Hazardous Trees

If a Maryland-licensed tree expert or certified arborist determines a tree is hazardous, it can be removed promptly. The usual permit steps don't hold up a genuine safety issue.
